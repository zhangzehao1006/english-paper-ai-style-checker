# Academic overrides

Apply these rules alongside `../references/claim-directness.md` before using
`../vendor/SKILL.md` (blader/humanizer v2.8.0, pinned at 9600f2b7). Read the
shared reference in full; it governs qualifications, scope, and directness and
takes precedence over conflicting vendor advice or examples. The vendor remains
unmodified.

## Preserve the argument, improve its expression

Academic prose can be formal, neutral, direct, and natural at the same time.
Preserve the author's voice and the scientific meaning. Do not import the
vendor's casual examples or its “PERSONALITY AND SOUL” instructions. Do not add
opinions, first person, colloquialism, colorful detail, or new evidence.

Do not assume that a hedge, passive construction, logical connective, long word,
dash, or three-item list is an AI tell. Assess its purpose and the surrounding
argument. Remove formulaic padding and unnecessary defensive framing when the
evidence and context support the proposed wording.

## Protected content

Preserve the following exactly when reviewing or revising prose:

- Statistics, numbers, dates, units, effect sizes, sample sizes, confidence
  intervals, symbols, and inequality signs.
- In-text citations and their attachment to the supported claims; complete
  reference entries, DOI links, and URLs.
- Direct quotations, formulas, variable names, technical terms and abbreviations,
  instruments, scales, tools, models, institutions, and other proper names.
- Range en-dashes, including `pp. 12–18`, `2019–2021`, `N = 30–45`, and
  `Grades 3–6`.

Also preserve substantive uncertainty and boundaries of inference. Their exact
wording may be improved, but their effect on interpretation must remain. Do not
delete a population restriction, causal caveat, model assumption, or uncertainty
statement merely because it sounds cautious. Do not add a citation, fact, data
point, or unreported result to repair vague prose. If information is missing,
identify the gap in the review.

## Additional review for excessive defensiveness

Use `../references/claim-directness.md` for the decision rules and examples.
Check all of the following, even when a sentence has only one hedge or none:

1. **Misplaced uncertainty:** an established observation is presented as a
   possibility. Distinguish this from an uncertain estimate, interpretation,
   explanation, or generalization before proposing a direct statement.
2. **Repeated scope restrictions:** the same boundary is reasserted in adjacent
   sentences or throughout a section without changing the reader's
   interpretation. Retain a clear statement where it governs the relevant
   claims; do not detach claims from that scope.
3. **Pre-emptive disclaimers:** a passage repeatedly denies interpretations that
   the argument does not invite. Keep denials needed to avoid a plausible
   misreading, including unsupported causal inference.
4. **Self-undermining framing:** “merely,” “only,” “at best,” or similar wording
   depreciates a contribution without adding information. Retain these words
   when they encode a real quantitative or logical restriction.
5. **Repeated limitations:** the same limitation is restated without a new
   consequence. Consolidate where appropriate, while retaining necessary
   boundaries in independently read text such as abstracts and captions.
6. **Accumulated defensive structure:** individually reasonable qualifications
   consume the paragraph and obscure its claim. State the supported result,
   attach the necessary boundary, then explain its implication without adding
   another generic disclaimer.

A direct sentence is not automatically a stronger scientific claim. Conversely,
deleting a small hedge can materially strengthen a claim. Evaluate the
proposition and evidence, not the word alone. If the source does not resolve the
distinction, retain the qualification and identify the uncertainty in the review.

## Audit the proposed edit

The vendor audit question “What makes this AI generated?” is replaced by:
“Which wording is formulaic, redundant, unnecessarily defensive, or unclear,
and what justified edit improves it without changing the scientific claim?”

Do not identify legitimate uncertainty, method passive, a useful connective,
technical vocabulary, or punctuation alone as a problem. Conversely, do not
exempt unnecessary uncertainty merely because it is customary in academic text.
Re-check the logic and scope after each proposed deletion or consolidation.

Word count, paragraph count, punctuation count, and edit percentage are
diagnostic information only. There is no mandatory 10–25% change rate, 25%
warning, 50% rollback rule, or grade based on these counts. An explicit word
limit or structural requirement from the user or venue still applies.

## Self-check

1. **Evidence and scope:** the edit preserves the observed result, inferential
   status, causal status, target population, and necessary boundaries. It neither
   strengthens nor weakens claims by default.
2. **Protected content:** statistics, citations, references, quotations,
   terminology, formulas, names, and numeric ranges match the original; citation
   attachment remains valid.
3. **Directness:** any removed hedge, caveat, or repeated limitation is redundant
   or misplaced in context; necessary uncertainty remains clearly attached.
4. **Register and voice:** academic formality and the author's voice remain;
   passive voice and punctuation serve the sentence rather than a quota.
5. **Completeness:** no distinct variable, outcome, argument, or limitation was
   lost while simplifying wording or a list.
6. **No invention:** no new fact, citation, result, explanation, or agent was
   introduced. An unresolved evidence question remains explicit in the review.

Repair or undo any edit that fails a check. Do not claim verification of content
you have not compared with the source.
